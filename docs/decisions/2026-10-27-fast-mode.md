# An opt-in fast mode (2026-10-27)

### Question

Fast mode (opt-in, not scenedetect-exact): what should happen?

The 2026-10-21 profile put 45-87% of CPU in decoding and up to 40% in deinterlacing (1080i). A mode that gives up
scenedetect's exact results could skip some of that work. Four candidates were measured, each changing some output:
FFmpeg's `lowres` decoding, h264 without its loop filter (`skip_loop_filter`), one field instead of yadif, and scoring
on luma without BGR/HSV.

### Choices

1. **Keep Maybe, record results.**
2. **Build `--fast`**: one field instead of yadif, plus `lowres` on MPEG-1/2, MPEG-4 Part 2 and MJPEG, after a check on
   a real off-air recording.
3. **Drop it.**

### Answer

**Keep Maybe, record results** (the owner, 2026-10-27).

### Setup

- A scratch copy of main, never pushed, with each variant switched on in VideoReader.
- The ffmpeg-executable path (`--decoder process --sequential`) with BtbN's FFmpeg 8.1.3 (CI's pin), on 4 vCPUs.
- `lowres` and one-field resize to the exact run's analysis grid, so the detectors see frames of the same size.
- Speed-ups are best of 3 interleaved adaptive runs, wall and CPU.
- Cut movement compares each variant against the exact run, per detector: cuts moved by 1-2 frames, lost and new.

Test files:

- Tears of Steel (4 min) encoded as MPEG-2 1080i25 TS, 576i25 MXF and 1080p25 TS, and as h264 1080p (crf 20): 39-44
  cuts each.
- The samples from `tools/make-samples.ps1`, including `broadcast/xdcam_hd422_1080i25.mxf`. That clip has no cuts, so
  it gives speed only.

Measuring the luma speed-up:

- It was measured on the ffmpeg pipeline alone: `format=gray` against today's bicubic `format=bgr24`.
- CPU saved: 7% on 1080i (yadif included), 25% on 1080p MPEG-2, 9% on h264 1080p and 4% on 576i.
- Skipping HSV in C# would save a few percent more.
- Its cut movement is close to that of `-l` on today's BGR (HSV's V only). So the lost cuts come from dropping hue and
  saturation, not from Y against V.

`lowres=1` gained nothing on progressive 1080p, and this wasn't explained. `lowres=2` on the same file gave 1.9x.

### Recommendation

Only **one field + `lowres`** is a candidate.

- **Gain:** about 2-2.8x on broadcast MPEG-2 and interlaced video.
- **Cut movement:**
  - Adaptive (the default): no cuts moved on the long files, 0-2 on the 480p samples.
  - Content: up to about 15% of cuts changed, mostly by one frame, a few lost.
- **One field** is the cleanest of all. It replaces yadif, which is already a departure from scenedetect, at about 2x
  with almost no movement.
- **h264 and HEVC** have no `lowres`, so progressive h264 (most files) gains nothing. The mode would serve only MPEG-2
  broadcast and interlaced material.

Dropped:

- **`skip_loop_filter`**: 10-17% faster, and it adds cuts that aren't there, even with adaptive.
- **Luma only**: 4-25% faster in the pipeline, and it changes what the detector measures. It misses cuts that change
  colour more than brightness. Anyone who wants it already has `-l`.

### Caveats

- The interlaced test files were made from 24p film. A cut can therefore fall between a frame's two fields, and real
  camera interlace may behave differently.
- Not measured: the in-process decoder (the default), chunked parallel runs and ARM.
- Revisit with a real off-air TS or MXF that has cuts before building anything.
